# How to use TREVV: startups, projects and teamwork

This guide is also available inside TREVV under **Getting started**. Work is saved to the server in live mode. Use one organization for a shared company membership boundary, workspaces for startups/businesses/clients, and project plans inside each workspace.

## Start on Dashboard

Dashboard is the workspace home. The former separate Overview entry has been removed; existing workspace links still open the same workspace, now on Dashboard. Use the project and people filters to choose a scope, click a status, number or person to inspect the matching work below, and use Clear chart filter to return to all work. Choose **Work due on** beneath the deadline chart to inspect a particular date. Project bars and milestone links open the original boards and items. Deadline windows change the upcoming-deadline chart; they do not hide older or undated work from the workspace totals. Workload shows open assignment counts, not an estimate of people's capacity.

In live workspaces, the top **Dashboard sections** tabs change the content below
without leaving Dashboard. **Summary** keeps the charts and source-work filters.
Choose **My Work**, **Projects**, **Teams and people**, **Messages**, **Inbox**,
**Attention**, **Decisions**, **Approvals** or **Waiting** to use those tools here.
Each section has an **Open full page** link for its dedicated workspace page.
The operating-loop cards also select the related dashboard section.

Section filters and drafts stay in place while switching tabs. Arrow keys move
between tabs; Home and End select the first and last section. The selected
section is included in the page URL, so reloading, bookmarking, and browser Back
and Forward restore it. Project and people filters under Summary apply to its
charts and source work; each other section keeps its own workspace scope.

## 1. Organize your startups and projects

1. Use a workspace for each startup, business or client. Portfolio brings those workspaces together within your organization.

2. Create a workspace from Portfolio. Give it a recognizable name, purpose and lead. Keep each project inside it as a project plan; use separate workspaces when access should differ.

3. Open Projects & sprints to create a project with a clear goal and target date. Existing boards remain available as project plans.

## 2. Create teams and add people

1. Choose Create Team, name it, and pick Marketing, Technology, Sales, Operations, Leadership or Custom. Select existing people and a lead, then create the team.

2. Open Manage. People is the first tab. Use Add an existing person for someone who already has workspace access, or enter their email under Invite someone to this team.

3. An organization owner or admin sends the invitation. The recipient follows the email, signs in or creates their account, and accepts. Acceptance adds them to the selected workspace, team and its conversation.

4. Refresh people after acceptance. Pending invitations are not assignable people. Check invitation delivery status and resend failed email from View invitations.

### Use the full Team page

In a live workspace, click a team's name or **Open team** in the directory. The
full page brings its work and conversations together. **Manage** still opens
the quick management drawer, which also links to the full page.

- **Overview:** review the team's purpose, lead, deadlines, blockers, workload
  and recent work. Click a progress card or a person to inspect matching tasks.
- **Tasks:** search and filter team work, switch between list and board views,
  update status, or open a task to edit its details and add updates/evidence.
  **New task** preselects this team and keeps a separate recoverable draft.
- **Projects & milestones:** create or edit projects, goals and timelines;
  plan dated sprints or cycles; add milestones and open the underlying work.
- **Communication:** send team updates and replies, mark the room as read, or
  choose **Topics and discussions** for a named discussion. Drafts survive
  section changes and reloads. **Open team room** opens the full Messages tools.
- **People & workload:** search members, view their assigned work, open a direct
  conversation, manage roles and membership, and invite people when authorized.
- **Settings:** edit the profile, department and tools. Unsaved profile edits
  stay available while moving between sections.

Each section has its own link, so it can be bookmarked or shared with someone
who has access. Explicit task or project ownership takes precedence over shared
team membership, keeping another team's assigned work out of these totals.

## 3. Give a project its team and context

1. Choose New project / plan, select its team, and optionally start from a department template.

2. For Marketing, record the campaign audience, channels and success metric. For IT, record the product or sprint goal, acceptance criteria, risks and definition of done.

3. Save the plan and open it. Its tasks, delivery cycles and milestones belong together; the team’s Projects and work tab links back to those plans.

## 4. Create and assign a task

Use the **Quick capture** (+) button in the workspace toolbar, or press **Q**,
to capture any supported work item. Choose **Task**, **Idea**, **Decision** or
**Approval** in the tabs. **Other items** opens **Milestone** and **Request**.
The save button follows your selected type. Arrow keys move between tabs;
Home and End jump to the first and last tab.

Switching types keeps your title, context, owner, priority and date. In live
workspaces, closing and reopening Quick capture also restores your draft.
Choose **Optional: save to Inbox for later** when you want to organize the
item later; its type and suggested assignment are kept for conversion to work.
Team-specific and issue follow-up captures retain their starting context.

1. Open the project and choose New task / work item. Enter an actionable title, expected outcome, priority and due date. Task creation goes directly to the project; Inbox is optional.

2. Choose an assignee. Filter people by team if useful. If someone is missing, use Add people to assign work, send an invitation, and refresh after they accept.

3. Use planning fields for the responsible team, work category, topic, sprint, milestone and relevant department details. Keep one accountable owner clear even when several people contribute.

4. Save, then open the task to edit its owner, dates, status and context. The assignee finds it in All my work across their workspaces and My Work within this workspace.

## 5. Plan sprints and project milestones

1. Open a project and choose Plan a sprint / cycle. Set its goal, start date and target date, then save it as Planned. Use a campaign or operations cycle when that fits the team better.

2. Assign project tasks to the cycle using each task’s planning fields. Start the cycle by editing its state to Active. Its board shows the selected work and completion progress.

3. Create a work item of type Milestone for each important deliverable, with an owner and date. Link contributing tasks through their Milestone field.

4. At the end, review completed and unfinished work. Mark the cycle Completed and move unfinished tasks to the next cycle through their planning fields. Tasks and their history remain in the parent project.

## 6. Keep communication with its topic

1. Open Manage → Topics and discussions. Create a named topic such as September campaign, Checkout redesign or Customer onboarding, and add the context or question.

2. Select a topic and reply in its discussion. These messages also appear in the team room, with the topic title. Team membership controls who can read and participate.

3. Use Messages for team rooms, private rooms and direct conversations. Create a separate room when a topic needs a different set of participants.

4. Post task-specific progress in the task’s Updates and evidence area. In Messages, use a request or decision with a response owner and due date when a reply must be followed up.

## 7. Run a daily follow-up

1. Start with Today and Overdue in My Work. Update Working, Review or Done as the work changes. Use the board view to see work by status.

2. If blocked, record the reason on the task. If waiting on someone, create a Waiting follow-up with a person and follow-up date; reschedule or resolve it as the situation changes.

3. A team lead checks Teams → member workload for open, overdue and blocked work. Reassign ownership or change the deadline explicitly when priorities change.

4. Check Attention for work that needs movement and Messages for requests needing a response. Record the next concrete action on the task or discussion.

### Work through an Attention issue

Click an issue's title or card to open its detail overlay. **Resolve** opens the
appropriate workflow: task actions for work issues, or a publishing form for a
workspace that needs an update. It does not immediately close the issue.

- **Issue details** explains the recommendation, owner, deadline, source
  evidence and detection history. Source and detection details remain available.
- **Task & resolution** keeps assignment, dates, status, updates, evidence,
  completion and history together. Decision/approval outcomes and existing
  waiting follow-ups appear when relevant.
- **Follow-up task** creates assignable work with the issue's context and link
  already included. The original task remains available in the overlay.
- **Communication** sends a private message or a message to an existing
  conversation. Requests and decisions can name a response owner and deadline.
  **Email draft** prepares a recipient, subject and body to review in your mail
  app; TREVV does not mark that draft as delivered.
- **Workspace update** publishes a review and saved workspace update for a
  stale-update issue.

After addressing the cause, expand **Record an outcome, snooze, or dismiss**.
Add the outcome or reason, then confirm the action. Snooze lasts 24 hours from
the action time. Creating a task or sending a message leaves the issue open.
If confirmation is missing, **Load latest** checks whether the issue remains
active; retries keep the same request identity. Conflicts allow reviewing or
reapplying to the latest version. Outcome, follow-up and communication drafts
are kept on this browser. The issue URL can be bookmarked or shared with
someone who has access.

## 8. Review progress every week

1. Review project milestones, cycle completion, overdue tasks and blockers with the team. Record decisions and approval outcomes in their dedicated views.

2. Publish a Weekly Review with wins, current priority, blockers, the next milestone and help needed. Use that record to prepare the next cycle.

3. Use Calendar for task deadlines and scheduled events, including recurring meetings. Recurring calendar events do not automatically create recurring tasks.

4. Return to Portfolio to compare the startups and workspaces you can access. Repeat the daily and weekly routines within each business context.

## Example: Marketing and IT working on the same launch

Create Marketing and Technology teams inside the startup workspace. Give Marketing a campaign project with audience, channel and success metrics. Give Technology a product project with sprint goals, components, environments and acceptance criteria. Create a named launch discussion in each team; use a separate shared room for the cross-team handoff. Create milestones for campaign approval and release readiness, then link each team’s tasks to its project milestone. Review the work from the project or team views and keep the founder’s weekly update in Weekly Review.

## Practical limits

- A pending invitation becomes an assignable person after acceptance. Failed email delivery is shown in Invitations and can be retried.
- Adding a person to a team requires workspace access first. The team invitation flow grants that access on acceptance. Owners and admins invite new people; team leads manage existing team members.
- Department categories and context fields organize work; they do not grant permissions or automatically integrate advertising, source control, CRM or external tools.
- Completing a sprint leaves unfinished work visible. Move each unfinished task to the next cycle; its task identity and history remain in the parent project.
- Recurring calendar events schedule meetings, not automatically repeated tasks. Waiting supplies explicit follow-up dates and actions.
- Background delivery depends on the deployed worker being awake. The existing Free hosting limitations remain separate from these product changes.

## Include people in plans and ideas

When creating a plan or capturing an Idea directly to a board, choose an optional
related team and turn on **Include people in a shared discussion**. Pick individual
people or select the available team members, and add a note describing the input
you want. You are included automatically. Leaving sharing off creates the item
without announcing it.

The selected people receive an unread announcement in **Messages**. Open the
linked discussion to reply, agree on next steps, or manage participants; use
**Open linked plan/idea** to return to the work. Workspace access remains unchanged.

Find these cards on **Dashboard → Plans and ideas**, Dashboard Summary, the live
**Ideas** page, and the relevant **Team overview**. **My Work** also shows your
assigned ideas and plans or ideas whose discussions include you. Existing cards
have **Include people** or **New discussion**, so you can invite input later.

If sharing is interrupted, the item remains saved. Use **Retry sharing** in the
sharing notice, including after reloading; it resumes the same request without
creating duplicate work or announcements. Inbox-first ideas remain available:
organize them on a board before inviting collaborators.
